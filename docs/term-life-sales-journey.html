Harbor Term Life sales journey

A customer buys a simple term-life policy with optional riders. This spec maps the screens, the facts we record, and how every library capability is used — from the first offer until Buy.

1. Purpose

Enrolment already proves the library on a classroom example. This sample is a second bounded context: a direct-to-consumer term-life quote and bind. It is meant to be implemented as a sibling Gradle module (harbor) that depends only on dcb-core for domain code and on dcb-postgres for persistence tests.

The app must exercise the whole library, not a subset:

Library pieceWhere this sample uses it
Facts + about / subjects Every recorded change. Several facts are about more than one subject.
question / projection Decision inputs and read models share the same definitions.
requiring vs considering Quote mutations lock quote history; the published rate card is only considered.
decision / unless / then One decision per customer action on a screen.
ConcurrencyConflict Two tabs on the same quote, or two buyers taking the last promo seat.
Ad-hoc ask / project { lookingAt } Each screen is rebuilt just in time from the quote’s facts.
Async projectAsync A sales pipeline and an issued-policy book, checkpointed in Postgres.
catchUpTo After Buy, the confirmation page waits for the pipeline to see the issue fact.
Postgres event store + tag table Integration tests run the same Given/When/Then against Docker Postgres.
Given / When / Then The acceptance spec below is the test list. An SME should be able to read it.

Synchronous projections (fold in the same transaction as append) are not in the library yet. This sample does not invent them. Screens use ad-hoc reads; dashboards use async catch-up.

2. Reasonable assumptions

These are product decisions for the sample, not claims about real insurance. Keep them small enough that a weekend implementation can finish the happy path.
  • Product. One product: Harbor Term Life. Level term, level premium, face amount paid on death during the term.
  • Term. 10, 20, or 30 years.
  • Face amounts. $100,000 · $250,000 · $500,000 · $1,000,000.
  • Riders (optional, simple). Accidental death (ADB, extra $ face on accidental death), waiver of premium (premiums waived if the insured is disabled), children’s term (flat $10,000 per child until age 25).
  • Eligibility. Age 18–60. Waiver only if age ≤ 55. Children’s term only if face ≥ $250,000. $1,000,000 face is not offered to tobacco users in this sample (simplified underwriting).
  • Pricing. A published rate card (facts) gives monthly premium per term × face × tobacco. Each rider has a flat monthly extra. The quote stores the priced premium when it is calculated, so later rate-card edits do not silently change a quote the customer already saw.
  • Promo. Campaign “Harbor Spring” has 100 discounted binds. Taking a promo seat is a contended DCB boundary, like a course filling up.
  • One open quote per customer. Starting a new journey abandons the previous open quote. A customer may hold at most one in-force Harbor policy in this sample.
  • No medical exam, no payment processor, no illustration PDF. “Buy” records that the customer confirmed the quote. Billing and underwriting casework are out of scope.
  • Identity. The sample pretends the customer is already signed in. We are given a CustomerId.

3. Journey map

The customer is led through six screens. Each screen is one command (sometimes two). They can go back and change a previous choice until they click Buy. After Buy the quote is bound and the journey ends.

1 Offer 2 Coverage 3 Riders 4 About you 5 Review 6 Confirm Buy

Going back does not delete history. A later fact supersedes an earlier one of the same kind (the question fold keeps the latest). That is how DCB stays aggregate-free: there is no Quote entity to mutate, only facts about the quote.

5. Facts

Stored type is the class simple name. about is the DCB tag set. A fact that names two subjects is one fact, not two streams and a saga.

FactAboutWhen
ProductDefined product Seed. Name, allowed terms, allowed faces.
RateCardPublished product Seed / ops. Versioned table of monthly premiums.
RiderCatalogued product, rider Seed. Code, extra premium, eligibility notes.
CampaignOpened campaign, product Seed. Discount, capacity (100).
JourneyStarted quote, customer Customer lands on the offer screen. Opens the quote.
QuoteAbandoned quote, customer A newer journey for the same customer supersedes this one.
PackagePicked quote Offer screen. One of the three starter packages, or “build your own”.
CoverageChosen quote Term years + face amount.
RiderAdded / RiderRemoved quote, rider One rider change. Cross-tagged so we can ask “is ADB on this quote?” without loading the whole quote.
ApplicantDescribed quote, customer Age, tobacco, sex. Needed to price and to enforce eligibility.
QuotePriced quote, product Monthly premium and the rate-card version used. The number the customer saw.
BeneficiaryNamed quote Primary beneficiary name and share (100% in the sample).
PromoApplied quote, campaign Customer opted into Harbor Spring on the review screen.
PolicyIssued policy, quote, customer, campaign? Buy. One fact binds the quote, creates the policy, and (if promo) takes a campaign seat.

PolicyIssued is the DCB “one fact” moment: the quote is bound, the customer now holds a policy, and the promo campaign has one fewer seat. There is no saga and no compensating “un-reserve” event on the happy path.

6. Screens

Screen 1 · Offer

Choose a starting point

Headline: “Protect the people who depend on you.” Three cards plus “I’ll choose the details”:

  • Essential — 10 years · $250,000 · no riders
  • Family — 20 years · $500,000 · ADB + children’s term
  • Long view — 30 years · $500,000 · ADB + waiver

First visit records JourneyStarted. Picking a card records PackagePicked and the implied CoverageChosen / rider facts in the same append (one decision, several facts). “I’ll choose” only records PackagePicked(custom) and goes to coverage.

ad-hoc Rebuild: is there already an open quote for this customer? If yes, offer “Resume” vs “Start over” (start-over abandons the old quote).

Screen 2 · Coverage

Term and amount

Two controls: term (10 / 20 / 30) and face (four amounts). Helper text from the product definition (allowed combinations). Changing coverage after riders exist may make a rider illegal; the decision rejects with a sentence the UI can show (“Children’s term needs at least $250,000 of coverage”).

requiring quote history, product definition. considering current rate card (to preview a premium, not to lock it).

Screen 3 · Riders

Optional extras

Three toggles from the rider catalogue. Disabled toggles show why (“Waiver is available up to age 55 — tell us your age on the next screen” if age is still unknown; hard-reject if age is known and too high).

Each toggle is its own decision: addRider / removeRider. That keeps the consistency boundary to this quote + this rider, so adding ADB does not contend with an unrelated quote adding waiver.

Screen 4 · About you

Who is being insured

Age, tobacco, legal sex (pricing class only). This is ApplicantDescribed. After this fact, eligibility that depends on age becomes enforceable. If waiver is already selected and age is 57, the decision rejects until they remove the rider or correct the age — we do not silently drop the rider.

Screen 5 · Review

The number they will pay

The screen always re-prices with the latest rate card (considering the card) and then records QuotePriced so the displayed premium is a fact. Optional checkbox: apply Harbor Spring (5% off, limited seats). That records PromoApplied but does not take a seat yet — seats are taken at Buy, so browsing does not hoard inventory.

Ad-hoc view: coverage, riders, applicant, premium, promo flag. This is a composed project { lookingAt … answer }.

Screen 6 · Confirm and Buy

Beneficiary, then bind

Name the primary beneficiary, then a single primary button: Buy — $X / month. Copy restates term, face, riders, and that coverage starts when we record the purchase (no medical exam in this sample).

buyPolicy is the wide decision: quote must be complete and still open, customer must not already hold Harbor, promo seat must still exist if they opted in, eligibility must still hold. On success: PolicyIssued (and the UI waits on catchUpTo before showing the confirmation page).

7. Decisions

Each function below is a decision { }. Rejection text is a sentence a screen can show. Commands never mention streams or aggregates.

7.1 Start or resume

// Offer screen, first arrival or “Start over”
fun startJourney(customer: CustomerId, quote: QuoteId) = decision {
  val already by requiring { quoteExists(quote) }
  val open    by requiring { openQuoteFor(customer) }
  decide {
    unless(!already) { "Quote $quote is already started" }
    // If they have an open quote, the same append abandons it.
    open?.let { then(QuoteAbandoned(it, customer)) }
    then(JourneyStarted(quote, customer))
  }
}

JourneyStarted is about the quote and the customer. Uniqueness of quote uses requiring { quoteExists } with no prior matches (create). The customer’s open quote is a second query item on the same decision — that is the dynamic boundary.

7.2 Pick a package

One decision, several facts. Family package appends coverage plus RiderAdded(adb) and RiderAdded(child). Custom package appends only PackagePicked.

7.3 Choose coverage

fun chooseCoverage(quote: QuoteId, termYears: Int, face: Int) = decision {
  val journey  by requiring { journeyOf(quote) }
  val product  by requiring { productDefinition(HarborTerm) }
  val riders   by requiring { ridersOn(quote) }
  val applicant by requiring { applicantOf(quote) }
  val rates    by considering { currentRateCard(HarborTerm) }
  decide {
    unless(journey.isOpen) { "This quote is no longer open" }
    unless(termYears in product.terms) { "Harbor Term is not offered for $termYears years" }
    unless(face in product.faces) { "Harbor Term is not offered at that amount" }
    unless(riders.all { it.allowed(termYears, face, applicant) }) {
      "A selected rider is not available with this coverage"
    }
    unless(applicant?.tobacco != true || face < 1_000_000) {
      "Tobacco users can choose up to $500,000 in this sample"
    }
    then(CoverageChosen(quote, termYears, face))
  }
}

The rate card is considered: we may show an estimated premium, but a concurrent rate-card publish must not bounce the coverage choice. Eligibility that the customer already asserted (riders, tobacco) is required.

7.4 Add or remove a rider

Boundary is quote + rider. Adding ADB on quote A does not conflict with adding waiver on quote B. Adding ADB twice on the same quote is rejected (“Accidental death is already on this quote”).

7.5 Describe the applicant

Requires the open journey and current riders. If waiver is selected and age > 55, reject. Does not lock the rate card.

7.6 Price the quote

Requires a complete quote (coverage + applicant). Considers the latest RateCardPublished. Appends QuotePriced(monthly, cardVersion). Re-visiting review after a change simply prices again; the fold keeps the latest price.

7.7 Buy

fun buyPolicy(
  quote: QuoteId,
  customer: CustomerId,
  policy: PolicyId,
  campaign: CampaignId? = CampaignId("spring"),
) = decision {
  val journey   by requiring { journeyOf(quote) }
  val coverage  by requiring { coverageOf(quote) }
  val applicant by requiring { applicantOf(quote) }
  val priced    by requiring { latestPrice(quote) }
  val beneficiary by requiring { beneficiaryOf(quote) }
  val inForce   by requiring { inForceHarborPolicy(customer) }
  val policyTaken by requiring { policyExists(policy) }
  val promo     by requiring { promoOn(quote) }
  val seats     by requiring { campaignSeatsTaken(campaign) }
  val campaignDef by requiring { campaignOf(campaign) }
  decide {
    unless(journey.isOpen && journey.customer == customer) { "This quote is not yours to buy" }
    unless(coverage != null) { "Choose a term and amount first" }
    unless(applicant != null) { "Tell us about the person being insured" }
    unless(priced != null) { "Review the premium before buying" }
    unless(beneficiary != null) { "Name a beneficiary" }
    unless(inForce == null) { "You already have a Harbor Term policy" }
    unless(!policyTaken) { "That policy number is already used" }
    unless(!promo || seats < campaignDef.capacity) {
      "Harbor Spring is fully subscribed"
    }
    then(PolicyIssued(policy, quote, customer, priced.monthly, campaign.takeIf { promo }))
  }
}

If two customers hit Buy on the last promo seat, one ConcurrencyConflicts (or the seat check fails on retry) and the UI drops the discount and offers full price. That is the same pattern as a full course in enrolment, with a different glossary.

8. Projections

8.1 Ad-hoc — each screen

Screens do not keep session state in a cookie blob. They ask the store. 

fun EventStore.offerFor(customer: CustomerId): OfferScreen = project {
  val open    by lookingAt { openQuoteFor(customer) }
  val product by lookingAt { productDefinition(HarborTerm) }
  answer { OfferScreen(openQuote = open, packages = product.packages) }
}.state

fun EventStore.reviewOf(quote: QuoteId): ReviewScreen = project {
  val coverage  by lookingAt { coverageOf(quote) }
  val riders    by lookingAt { ridersOn(quote) }
  val applicant by lookingAt { applicantOf(quote) }
  val price     by lookingAt { latestPrice(quote) }
  val promo     by lookingAt { promoOn(quote) }
  answer { ReviewScreen(coverage, riders, applicant, price, promo) }
}.state

After any command, the HTTP handler uses the accepted position only if it must wait on an async view. The next screen itself is ad-hoc and can be asked immediately from the event store (same process, the facts are already committed).

8.2 Async — pipeline and book of business

Two named projections, empty about so they see every relevant fact (same shape as enrolment’s courseDirectory).

NameStateUsed for
sales-pipeline Open quotes: step reached, age of quote, promo flag, last premium Internal desk. “Where do people drop off?”
policy-book Issued policies: customer, face, term, monthly, campaign Confirmation wait, “my policy” page, promo remaining-seat badge 
val pipeline = store.projectAsync("sales-pipeline", salesPipeline(), snapshots)
val book     = store.projectAsync("policy-book", policyBook(), snapshots)
pipeline.start()
book.start()

// After Buy:
val outcome = store.handle(buyPolicy(...))
val position = (outcome as Outcome.Accepted).position!!
book.catchUpTo(position)
// now the confirmation page may read book.state[policy]

Snapshots live in Postgres (PostgresProjectionStore) so a process restart continues from the last store head. rebuild() is the ops hatch if a fold is ever patched.

Remaining promo seats for a banner can be either: ad-hoc ask(campaignSeatsTaken(spring)) on the review screen (always exact), or the policy-book async view (fast, possibly a few milliseconds behind). The spec uses ad-hoc on the Buy path (must be exact) and the async book for the public “93 of 100 spring policies left” badge.

9. Acceptance tests (Given / When / Then)

These names are the test methods. An SME should be able to read them aloud.

// Offer
"a customer can start a journey"
"starting a second journey abandons the open quote"
"a quote id cannot be started twice"

// Coverage
"a customer can choose 20 years and $500,000"
"30 years is offered; 15 years is not"
"a tobacco user cannot choose $1,000,000"
"children's term cannot stay selected below $250,000"

// Riders
"accidental death can be added and removed"
"a rider cannot be added twice"
"waiver of premium is refused at age 57"

// Review / price
"review records the premium the customer was shown"
"a later rate card does not change a premium already recorded"

// Buy
"a complete quote can be bought"
"buy is refused when the beneficiary is missing"
"a customer cannot hold two Harbor policies"
"the last Harbor Spring seat can be taken only once"
"two buyers of the last seat: one binds, the other is told the promo is full"

The last case is the enrolment “course is full” test with different words. Implement it twice: in-memory, and .against(postgres). 

given(
  ProductDefined(HarborTerm, ...),
  RateCardPublished(HarborTerm, version = 1, ...),
  CampaignOpened(Spring, capacity = 1, discount = 0.05),
  JourneyStarted(q1, ada),
  /* ada’s complete quote + promo */
  JourneyStarted(q2, grace),
  /* grace’s complete quote + promo */
  PolicyIssued(p1, q1, ada, monthly = 28, campaign = Spring),
).whenever {
  buyPolicy(q2, grace, p2, Spring)
}.expectRejection("Harbor Spring is fully subscribed")

11. Out of scope

  • Medical underwriting, labs, MIB, e-signature, replacement forms.
  • Payment capture, billing retries, lapse, reinstatement.
  • Illustrations, tax, state variation, producer of record.
  • Synchronous projections (not in the library).
  • Multi-tenant partitioning (one store for the sample).

If the journey needs a “save for later” email, that is an automation on JourneyStarted + time, not a new consistency boundary.
